COMPANY RESEARCH RULES
Objective

Research companies provided by Irmantas and build deep, credible account intelligence that gives Teltonika Networks Australia multiple natural reasons to start a conversation.

The companies provided are already considered potential Teltonika prospects.

The main question is:

"What is happening at this company, who is involved, and what credible reasons does Irmantas have to contact them?"

The research should help Irmantas understand the account and decide himself which angle is most useful. Do NOT optimise the process around finding one "Best Trigger" as quickly as possible.

Do NOT focus on recommending individual Teltonika products. The objective is to start a relevant conversation.

0. Entity → Trading Name Check

Before the main research, check whether the supplied company name is a legal entity rather than the name the business trades under (e.g. "Milan Industries Group Pty Ltd" → Otto IT; "One Solution IT Pty Ltd" → 1Solution).

Verify the relationship using evidence such as:

ABN / ASIC details (entity name, registered location)
Matching street addresses
Websites and domains (including documents hosted on the trading-name website)
Matching phone numbers
ASN / IP registration records
Other reliable sources (company About pages, contracts, LinkedIn)

In the output:

State the trading name and the evidence linking it to the supplied entity.
Give a confidence level.
If the link cannot be confirmed, say so and do not merge the two names.

Then research under the trading name, since news, projects, people and case studies are usually published under it.

2. Research Depth

For every company, research broadly before selecting or ranking triggers.

Do not stop researching after finding one strong trigger.

Aim to find multiple relevant developments, projects, technologies, partnerships or other useful pieces of account intelligence.

Normal company: aim for approximately 3–7 useful findings when enough public information exists.
Large companies (e.g. Schneider Electric, Datacom, major transport/energy companies): research more deeply, because considerably more information may exist.

Quality still matters more than hitting a number. Do not include irrelevant corporate news simply to reach 3–7 findings.

Depth does NOT mean lowering the evidence threshold. 3 strong findings after extensive research are better than 7 weak findings created to fill the output.

Do not rely only on normal Google-style company news results. Do not stop at basic company information. Follow promising leads.

Councils, water utilities and other public-sector infrastructure organisations

For these organisations, the most useful information is often not in news but in their own governance documents. Actively search:

Council meeting agendas and minutes
Committee reports (e.g. Infrastructure Services, Water & Sewer, Corporate Services committees)
Infrastructure and works program reports
Budgets, Delivery Programs and Operational Plans (DPOP), Long Term Financial Plans
Capital works schedules and capital expenditure reports
Asset management plans and strategies

Look for references to SCADA, telemetry, communications, control systems, radio vs 4G/5G, pump stations, treatment plants, CCTV and other infrastructure upgrades.

Note the report author and responsible officer — they usually identify the relevant people (e.g. Manager Water & Sewer, Director Infrastructure Services).

Check the most recent agendas to confirm whether an item is still active, and whether budgeted items were actually adopted.

Strong

Infrastructure deployment

Never manufacture an outreach trigger simply because the company is a Teltonika prospect.

10. Research Behaviour
Search multiple sources where useful.
Follow promising findings deeper.
Prefer primary and credible industry sources.
Provide source links and dates.
Separate confirmed FACT from INFERENCE.
Never invent projects, technologies, suppliers, people's responsibilities or requirements.
Never manufacture Teltonika relevance.
If no strong recent trigger exists, say so.
Quality is more important than finding something for every company.
Source Priority

Make a strong effort to find primary or credible industry sources first:

Company, customer and partner announcements (newsrooms, ASX releases, press releases)
Government sources (agency project pages, tender and contract award notices, council agendas and committee reports)
Credible industry publications (e.g. iTnews, ARN, trade press)

Use aggregators such as Wikipedia, Crunchbase, PitchBook, ZoomInfo or Simply Wall St only as a last resort, and clearly flag when a finding relies on them.

No Speculative Developments

Do not surface developments that are only likely, expected or logical (e.g. "a re-tender is probably underway because the contract ends next year") unless there is evidence they are actually happening.

If something is worth watching but unconfirmed, it may be listed under Research Gaps — not as a finding or trigger.

11. Teltonika Perspective

Research through the lens of industrial/IoT connectivity and networking.

Do NOT attempt detailed product selection during initial research.

Technical requirements such as Ethernet ports, SIM/eSIM, VPN, serial interfaces, I/O, GNSS and protocols become relevant later if a specific opportunity is discovered.

Detailed relevance logic is maintained in 02_TELTONIKA_RELEVANCE.md.
