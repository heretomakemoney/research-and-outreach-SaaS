04_OUTREACH_RULES
 
## Purpose
 
Create personalized B2B outreach for Irmantas.
 
The emails must sound like Irmantas actually wrote them.
 
The objective is NOT to create the theoretically perfect cold sales email.
The objective is to create a good sales email in Irmantas' natural writing style.
 
Think:
 
IRMS' REAL WRITING STYLE
+
RESEARCH-LED PERSONALIZATION
+
SLIGHTLY STRONGER SALES LOGIC
 
Do not turn the email into generic SaaS/LinkedIn sales copy.
 
The purpose of the first email is to START A RELEVANT CONVERSATION — not to explain Teltonika or sell a router.
 
 
# 1. IRMANTAS' NATURAL EMAIL STYLE
 
Irmantas writes in a simple, direct and conversational way.
 
Typical characteristics:
 
- Short paragraphs
- Usually 1–2 sentences per paragraph
- Simple vocabulary
- Direct questions
- Friendly but not overly enthusiastic
- Professional without sounding formal
- Often starts immediately with the reason for contacting someone
- Often uses "How are you?" with existing contacts
- Frequently uses "As discussed..."
- Frequently uses "I would like to..."
- Frequently uses "Let me know..."
- Frequently asks "Would you be available...?"
- Often uses "catch up" instead of "meeting"
- Uses "quick chat", "quick catch up", "tech catch up"
- Uses "Cheers" or "Kind regards"
- Does not try to sound clever
 
The writing can be slightly imperfect/conversational.
 
Do NOT intentionally introduce spelling or grammar mistakes.
 
Improve grammar where necessary while preserving Irmantas' sentence structure and vocabulary.
 
 
# 2. DO NOT MAKE IRMANTAS SOUND LIKE A SALES BOT
 
Avoid classic cold-email language such as:
 
"I noticed your impressive growth..."
 
"Given your recent expansion..."
 
"I'd love to explore how Teltonika can help..."
 
"We help companies like yours unlock..."
 
"I came across your profile..."
 
"Would it be worth exploring synergies..."
 
"Could I steal 15 minutes of your time?"
 
"Are you the right person to discuss this with?"
 
"Sounds like this could be a perfect fit..."
 
"I wanted to reach out because..."
 
"I'd love to connect..."
 
These phrases may be grammatically good but generally do NOT sound like Irmantas.
 
Do not write like a LinkedIn sales influencer.
 
 
# 3. PERSONALIZATION
 
Personalization is important, but it must sound natural.
 
Use company research to provide Irmantas with a REAL REASON to contact the person.
 
Do not simply prove that research was performed.
 
BAD:
 
"I noticed that Company X recently announced the deployment of 250 EV chargers across NSW. Congratulations on this exciting milestone."
 
This sounds generated.
 
BETTER:
 
"I saw you are rolling out another 250 EV chargers across NSW."
 
Then naturally connect it to why Irmantas is contacting them.
 
Example:
 
"I saw you are rolling out another 250 EV chargers across NSW.
 
Not sure how you're currently handling connectivity for these sites, but I thought it could be worth having a chat."
 
This sounds much closer to Irmantas.
 
## Research intelligence is the main strength of the email
 
The specific intelligence found during research — technologies, existing vendors/competitors, projects, applications, events, posts by relevant employees — should carry the email.
 
Use it to ask a relevant, informed, commercially useful question.
 
The question should show that Irmantas understands something specific about how they work, and it should be something the recipient can actually answer.
 
Prefer:
 
"I noticed you're already working with cellular alongside radio for telemetry applications. I'm interested to understand what you're currently using for 4G/5G when cellular is either the primary connection or backup."
 
Over:
 
A generic connectivity question that could be sent to any company in the industry.
 
 
# 4. SLIGHTLY IMPROVE HIS SALES STYLE
 
Do not copy Irmantas' historical emails blindly.
 
They are examples of VOICE, not necessarily perfect sales methodology.
 
Improve them by:
 
- giving a clearer reason for contacting the prospect
- using stronger company-specific personalization
- making the relevance clearer
- removing unnecessary information
- asking a simple question that encourages a response
- occasionally creating curiosity rather than explaining everything
- using relevant credibility where useful
 
The improvement should be subtle.
 
Think approximately:
 
80–90% Irmantas
10–20% sales optimisation.
 
If an optimized sentence sounds unlike something Irmantas would actually say, simplify it.
 
 
# 5. FIRST CONTACT VS EXISTING RELATIONSHIP
 
## Cold / first contact
 
Do not pretend familiarity.
 
Usually avoid:
 
"How are you?"
 
unless it genuinely fits.
 
Start with the reason for contacting them.
 
Example:
 
Hi John,
 
I saw your team is working on the new monitoring rollout across regional NSW.
 
Not sure how you're handling connectivity at the remote sites, so I'm interested to understand whether that's cellular or something else.
 
I'm with Teltonika Networks and thought there could be some overlap.
 
Would you be available for a quick chat next week?
 
Cheers,
 
 
## Existing contact
 
More casual openings are encouraged:
 
"How are you?"
 
"Hope you are well."
 
"Good to catch up as always."
 
"Thanks for the call earlier."
 
"Great catching up earlier."
 
"Happy Friday!"
 
"As discussed..."
 
Use the relationship context.
 
 
# 6. SALES TRIGGERS
 
When a strong Sales Trigger exists, normally mention it early.
 
Keep it simple.
 
Do not explain their own announcement back to them.
 
BAD:
 
"I recently came across the exciting announcement that your organization has secured a contract to deploy..."
 
GOOD:
 
"I saw you recently won the contract for..."
 
Then explain why that caused Irmantas to contact them.
 
 
# 7. TELTONIKA INTRODUCTION
 
Do NOT explain Teltonika the same way in every email.
 
Do not default to a standard paragraph such as:
 
"We're a European manufacturer of industrial 4G/5G routers with a local Sydney office."
 
Often a short line is enough:
 
"I'm with Teltonika Networks."
 
"I'm with Teltonika Networks and thought there could be some overlap here."
 
Add more context about Teltonika only when it genuinely helps the recipient understand the relevance (e.g. the recipient is unlikely to know what Teltonika makes, or local supply/support is actually relevant to their situation).
 
When more context is needed, keep it factual and short, as Irmantas naturally writes:
 
"We are a European manufacturer..."
 
"We have a local Sydney office for supply and technical support."
 
Do not write marketing descriptions such as:
 
"Teltonika is a global leader in cutting-edge industrial connectivity solutions."
 
Avoid dumping the entire portfolio.
 
Only mention products when they strengthen the reason for contacting the person.
 
## Channel / commercial structure
 
Do NOT mention the channel partnership model (or other commercial/partner structures) in initial outreach just because the company is an integrator, reseller, distributor or potential partner.
 
This is normally irrelevant to the recipient and is not a reason for them to speak with Irmantas.
 
Only mention it when it is specifically relevant to the conversation (e.g. they enquired about becoming a partner, or the discussion is already about supply/partnership terms).
